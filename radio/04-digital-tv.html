4. Digital TV (ISDB-Tb): mapping the UHF channels

What you can and cannot do

Possible with the V4L?
See the channels in the spectrum and measure their strength Yes
Find out which physical channels are active in your area Yes (this procedure)
Watch the picture No

Why you cannot watch the picture:

  • Bandwidth. Chile uses ISDB-Tb, with 6 MHz channels and a 5.57 MHz signal. The V4L sees at most about 2.4 MHz at a time.
  • The chip. The RTL2832U's built-in TV mode is DVB-T, the European standard, which is incompatible with ISDB-Tb. That mode was also replaced by WinUSB when the SDR driver was installed.
  • The practical alternative: to watch TV on the PC, use a USB ISDB-T tuner.

What a digital channel looks like in SDR#: a flat plateau with very sharp edges and small peaks on top (the TMCC/AC pilot carriers).

The channel formula

UHF channel n (14 to 51) occupies:

start = 470 + 6·(n − 14) MHz        end = start + 6 MHz
signal center = start + 3 + 1/7 MHz   (ISDB-Tb is offset by +143 kHz)

Example, channel 24: it spans 530 to 536 MHz and the signal is centered at 533.143 MHz. The 1seg segment (the mobile version, about 430 kHz wide) sits right at that center.

Step 1: close SDR# and SDR++

Step 2: sweep from 470 to 700 MHz (2 minutes)

From the project folder:

drivers/rtlsdrblog-V1.4.0/x64/rtl_power.exe -f 470M:700M:50k -g 12.5 -c 25% -i 10 -e 2m scans/tv_uhf.csv
Option Why
-f 470M:700M:50k The whole UHF TV band (channels 14–51), with 50 kHz resolution
-g 12.5 Moderate gain. The channels from Santiago's hilltop transmitters arrive strong. If the noise floor shows steps, go down to -g 8.7
-c 25% Discards the edges of each hop
-e 2m About 12 full sweeps, enough to average

Step 3: detect the channels

python tools/canales_tv.py scans/tv_uhf.csv 6
  • What it does: checks channels 14 to 51 and marks a channel as active when its central power is more than 6 dB above the noise floor (the last argument).
  • What it creates: the plot scans/tv_uhf_canales.png (active channels in green) and a table. Add --en to get a copy with English labels, scans/tv_uhf_canales_en.png.
  • The "Forma" (shape) column: tells whether the channel has the flat plateau with sharp edges typical of ISDB-Tb, or is irregular.

How to read the plot:

  • The thin peaks right next to the strong channels (for example at 528, 547 and 567 MHz) are not channels. They are sweep artifacts: images and hop edges from rtl_power when a very strong signal is nearby.
  • The physical channel number (24, 29...) is not the virtual number the TV shows (for example 7.1 or 13.1). To find out which station it is, check the TV's signal information menu, which usually shows the physical UHF channel.

Step 4 (optional): look at a channel in an SDR program

  1. Tune to the signal center: start + 3.143 MHz. For example, 563.143 MHz for channel 29.
  2. Set the Sample Rate to 2.4 MSPS and RF Gain to 0–10 dB. Strong channels overload easily.
  3. You will see the central part of the plateau. To see an edge, shift the frequency by about ±2.5 MHz.

Repeat it to compare

  • A different antenna position: save each sweep under a different name, for example scans/tv_ventana.csv (window) or scans/tv_exterior.csv (outdoors). That way you can compare how many dB you gain.
  • VHF channels (7–13, 174–216 MHz): change the range to -f 174M:216M:50k. The automatic table only covers UHF, but the graficar_power.py plot works the same.
